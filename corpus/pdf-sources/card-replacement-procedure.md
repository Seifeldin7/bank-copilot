# Debit Card Replacement Procedure
Synthetic training material. Not an official policy of any bank.
Document owner: Retail Cards Department
Version: 2
Effective date: 2026-03-01

## Purpose and scope

This procedure explains how branch employees of the Bank handle requests to replace a debit card. It applies to every branch and to all personal debit cards issued to retail customers. It covers damaged cards, demagnetised cards, cards that are close to expiry, design changes and lost cards. Stolen cards and the immediate blocking steps are covered in the separate Lost and Stolen Card Handling guide, and this procedure refers to that guide where needed.

## Who should use this document

Use this document if you serve customers at the counter, prepare service requests in the request system, or review requests as a supervisor. Employees prepare and submit requests. Supervisors approve or reject them. No card order may be placed until a supervisor has approved the request.

## Overview of the process

Every replacement follows the same basic path:

1. Identify the customer and verify identity with an original document.
2. Establish the reason for replacement and check that it is eligible.
3. Explain the fee, if any, and the expected waiting time.
4. Create a card service request with the customer reference, the last four digits of the card, the issue type and the delivery branch.
5. Submit the request for supervisor approval.
6. After approval, the request is executed once and the card order is placed.
7. Inform the customer when and where to collect the card.

## Key principles

- Only the last four digits of a card number are ever written in a request, note or message. Never record a full card number.
- Customer references use the format CUS-123456, for example CUS-204518.
- The delivery branch code uses the format CAI-012, three capital letters, a hyphen and three digits.
- The customer must collect the new card in person at the delivery branch.

## Eligibility for replacement

A debit card may be replaced when it is damaged, demagnetised, expired within the next 30 days, or when the customer requests a new design. The customer must present a valid national ID or passport at the branch. Replacement of a damaged card is processed within 5 working days.

The sections below give practical guidance for each eligible situation so that employees can classify the request correctly in the system.

## Damaged cards

A card is damaged when the chip, the body or the printed details are broken, scratched or worn so that the card no longer works reliably. Ask the customer to hand over the damaged card. The damaged card must be cut in front of the customer after the replacement request has been approved, not before.

## Demagnetised cards

If the magnetic stripe no longer reads at terminals but the card looks undamaged, treat it as a demagnetised card. 

## Cards close to expiry

A card that expires within the next 30 days may be replaced early. If the card has already expired, the replacement is also accepted. In both cases the replacement is free of charge, as explained on the next page.

## Design change

A customer may ask for a new card design while the existing card is still valid. This is an eligible reason, but it is not free. The fee for design changes is explained in the Replacement fees section.

## Identity checks

Before creating any request, verify the customer identity using an original national ID or passport. Compare the photo and signature with the Bank records. A photocopy or a photo on a phone is not acceptable. If the customer cannot present a valid document, do not create the request and explain which documents are accepted.

## Replacement fees

A replacement fee of EGP 50 applies to damaged or design-change replacements. Replacements caused by bank-side defects or expiry are free of charge. The fee is debited from the linked account after the request is approved.

The full list of card fees is in the Retail Fee Schedule 2026. This page explains how to apply the replacement fee correctly and how to talk to the customer about it.

## When the fee applies

The EGP 50 fee applies in these cases:

- The card is damaged through use, wear or accident.
- The customer asks for a new design while the existing card is still valid.

## When the replacement is free

No fee is charged in these cases:

- The card has a bank-side defect, for example a chip that was faulty when issued or a card printed with wrong details.
- The card has expired or expires within the next 30 days.

If you are unsure whether a defect is bank-side, describe what you observed in the request description and let the supervisor decide. Do not tell the customer that the fee will be waived until the supervisor has approved the request.

## How the fee is collected

The customer does not pay cash at the counter. The fee is debited from the linked account only after the supervisor approves the request. If the linked account does not hold enough balance on the day of approval, the system records the failed debit and the branch contacts the customer. The card order is not placed until the fee has been collected, unless the replacement is free.

## Explaining the fee to the customer

Tell the customer the amount before you submit the request. Use clear and neutral wording, for example: "A replacement fee of EGP 50 will be debited from your account once the request is approved." Record in the description that the fee was explained.

## Lost card replacement: required documents

For a lost card replacement the employee must confirm the card has been blocked, record the last four digits of the card, and capture the delivery branch. A replacement card for a lost card requires supervisor approval before it is issued. The new card is ready for collection within 7 working days.

This page lists the checks and records that apply only to lost cards. The blocking steps themselves are described in the Lost and Stolen Card Handling guide.

## Before you create the request

1. Verify the customer identity with an original national ID or passport.
2. Confirm that the card has been blocked. Ask for the blocking reference number or check the card management screen.
3. If the card is not yet blocked, stop and block it first. Blocking is permanent and the card cannot be reactivated.
4. Explain to the customer that the new card will have a new number and that any recurring payments must be updated by the customer.

## Information to record

- Customer reference, for example CUS-318842.
- Last four digits of the lost card only.
- Issue type: lost.
- Delivery branch code, for example CAI-012.
- Blocking reference number, in the description.

## Stolen cards

For stolen cards the same information is needed. If the customer reports disputed transactions, remind the customer to file a police report within 48 hours and record the police report reference in the notes when it is available.

## Supervisor approval

A replacement card for a lost card requires supervisor approval before it is issued. The employee prepares and submits the request but may never approve it. The supervisor reviews the blocking confirmation and the delivery branch, and then approves or rejects the request with a reason.

## Waiting time

The new card is ready for collection within 7 working days. Damaged cards are faster at 5 working days, so make sure you do not quote the damaged card timing to a customer with a lost card.

## Submitting the request and approval

All service requests need supervisor approval before execution. This page describes the final steps for every type of card replacement.

## Request fields

The card service request must contain:

- A title of at least 5 characters, for example "Damaged debit card replacement".
- A description of at least 10 characters that states the reason and the checks performed.
- The customer reference in the format CUS-123456.
- The last four digits of the card.
- The issue type.
- The delivery branch in the format CAI-012.

The system checks these fields before submission. Errors must be corrected. Warnings, such as a missing police report reference for a stolen card, should be read carefully and resolved where possible.

## Approval

After submission the request has the status pending approval. A supervisor may approve it, reject it, or correct it before approving. A supervisor who rejects a request must record a reason. A supervisor may not approve a request they submitted themselves. Every change is recorded in the request history.

## Execution

An approved request is executed once. If someone tries to execute it again, the system returns the original execution record and does not place a second card order. Do not create a duplicate request to work around this.

## Informing the customer

After execution, tell the customer the card order reference, the delivery branch and the expected waiting time: 5 working days for damaged cards and 7 working days for lost cards. Remind the customer to bring an original national ID or passport on collection.

## Checklist

- Identity verified with an original document.
- Reason and eligibility confirmed.
- Fee explained when it applies.
- Blocking confirmed for lost cards.
- Request submitted and approved by a supervisor.
- Customer informed about collection.
