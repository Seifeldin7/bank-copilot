Effective date: 2026-02-15

## Purpose and scope

This guide explains how branch employees of the Bank respond to customer questions about credit card limits and how limit increase requests are prepared. It applies to personal credit cards issued to retail customers, including classic, gold and platinum products.

The credit limit is the maximum amount a customer may owe on a card at any time. It is set by the credit department after assessing the customer's income, repayment history and existing obligations. Branch employees do not set, change or promise limits. Their role is to give accurate information, collect the right documents and submit requests correctly.

## Key terms

- Approved limit: the maximum amount the Bank has approved for the card.
- Available balance: the part of the approved limit that is not currently used or reserved.
- Limit increase request: a request, prepared at the branch and assessed by the credit department, to raise the approved limit.
- Current limit: the approved limit that is active on the day of the visit.

## What this guide covers

1. How to answer limit inquiries safely and correctly.
2. How to prepare a limit increase request and which documents are needed.
3. How supervisor approval and credit assessment work together.

## Principles for all limit conversations

- Always verify identity first, using an original national ID or passport.
- Be factual. Share only the information this guide allows.
- Never promise, hint at or estimate the outcome of a limit increase request.
- Record only the last four digits of the card and the customer reference, for example CUS-402291.
- Do not discuss another person's card, even for family members, unless the Bank has approved a power of attorney.

## Limit inquiry process

Customers may ask about their current credit card limit at any branch after identity verification. Employees may share the approved limit and available balance but may not promise a limit increase.

This page describes how to handle an inquiry from the first question to the record that closes it.

## Steps for a limit inquiry

1. Greet the customer and ask which card the question is about.
2. Verify identity with an original national ID or passport. Compare the photo and signature with the Bank records.
3. Open the card record and confirm the last four digits with the customer.
4. Tell the customer the approved limit and the available balance.
5. Answer follow-up questions about how the limit works, using the key terms from the previous page.
6. If the customer wants a higher limit, explain that a limit increase request can be prepared and assessed by the credit department, and move to the next page.
7. Record the inquiry in the customer notes with the date and the last four digits of the card.

## What employees may share

- The approved limit.
- The available balance.
- The general documents needed for a limit increase request.

## What employees must not do

- Promise or suggest that a limit increase will be granted.
- Quote an expected new limit or a percentage the customer is likely to receive.
- Share the limit with anyone who has not completed identity verification.
- Send limit information through personal messaging applications.

## Inquiries by phone or message

Branch employees do not give limit details by phone or over messaging applications, because the caller cannot be verified against an original document. Invite the customer to visit a branch or to use the secure channels of the Bank.

## Example

A customer, CUS-402291, asks whether her classic card limit can be raised. The employee verifies her ID, confirms card ending 4417, shares the approved limit and available balance, and explains that a request can be prepared. The employee does not say whether it will be accepted.

## Limit increase requests

A limit increase request requires the last three salary statements and is assessed by the credit department. Requests for increases above 50 percent of the current limit require additional income documentation.

The employee prepares the request, but the decision belongs to the credit department. The employee must never tell the customer that the request is approved before the Bank confirms it.

## Required documents

For every limit increase request collect:

- The last three salary statements.
- A copy of the original national ID or passport, checked against the original.
- The customer reference and the last four digits of the card.

## Increases above 50 percent

If the requested new limit is more than 50 percent above the current limit, the customer must also provide additional income documentation. Examples include an employer letter, a tax declaration, bank statements from other institutions, or proof of other regular income. The credit department may ask for further documents after review.

Worked examples:

- Current limit EGP 20,000, requested EGP 28,000: an increase of 40 percent. Three salary statements are enough.
- Current limit EGP 20,000, requested EGP 34,000: an increase of 70 percent. Additional income documentation is required.

An increase of exactly 50 percent does not need extra documentation.

## Preparing the request

1. Confirm the card, the current limit and the requested limit.
2. Calculate the percentage increase and decide whether extra documentation is needed.
3. Check that all documents are complete, readable and in the customer's name.
4. Create the request with a clear description, listing the documents received.
5. Submit the request for supervisor approval.

## Missing documents

If a document is missing, do not submit the request. Explain what is needed and invite the customer to return. Where the situation calls for it, record an additional documentation request with a due date no later than 14 days from the visit.

## Supervisor approval, assessment and customer communication

All service requests need supervisor approval before execution. A limit increase request is first approved by a supervisor at the branch, and only then is it passed to the credit department for assessment.

## The supervisor role

The supervisor checks that the documents are complete, that the percentage increase was calculated correctly and that the description is clear. The supervisor may correct a request before approving it, or reject it with a recorded reason. A supervisor may not approve a request they submitted themselves. Every change is recorded in the request history with the supervisor identity and timestamp.

## The credit department role

The credit department assesses income, repayment history and existing obligations. It may approve the full amount, approve a lower amount, or decline the request. Its decision is final at branch level. Branch employees do not have access to the scoring model and must not speculate about the reasons for a decision.

## Communicating with the customer

- After submission, tell the customer that the request will be assessed and that the Bank will notify them of the outcome.
- Do not give a fixed date for the decision unless the credit department has confirmed one.
- If the customer asks for a decision by a specific date, for example before travel, note the request in the description but make no commitment.
- If the request is declined, explain that the decision was taken by the credit department and offer to record a complaint if the customer wishes.

## Fees

A limit increase request carries no separate fee. Annual fees for credit cards are listed in the Retail Fee Schedule 2026. The annual fee for classic credit cards is EGP 300.

## Quick checklist

- Identity verified with an original document.
- Last three salary statements collected.
- Additional income documentation collected for increases above 50 percent.
- No promise made to the customer.
- Request approved by a supervisor before it goes to credit assessment.
