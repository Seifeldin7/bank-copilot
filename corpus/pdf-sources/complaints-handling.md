# Customer Complaints Handling Procedure
Synthetic training material. Not an official policy of any bank.
Document owner: Customer Experience Department
Version: 1
Effective date: 2025-04-15
## Purpose
This procedure sets out how branch employees of the Bank receive, record, investigate and close customer complaints. Its aim is to treat every complaint fairly, to respond within defined time limits, and to learn from complaints so that service improves.

The procedure applies to all branch staff, including tellers, customer service officers, supervisors and branch managers. It applies to complaints received in person, by telephone, by letter, by email, through the mobile app, and through social media messages addressed to the branch.

## What is a complaint
A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction by a customer about the Bank's products, services, staff or processes, where the customer expects a response or a correction. Examples include:
- A card that was not delivered within the promised time.
- A fee the customer believes was charged wrongly.
- Rude or unhelpful behaviour by a member of staff.
- An error in a statement or a transfer.
- Repeated failures of internet banking or the mobile app.

A simple question or a request for information is not a complaint. If the customer is unsure, the employee should treat the matter as a complaint and let the supervisor decide later.

## Principles
All employees must follow these principles:
1. Listen without interrupting and without defending the Bank.
2. Never discourage a customer from complaining.
3. Never argue, blame colleagues, or promise an outcome before the investigation is complete.
4. Never record full card or account numbers. Use the customer reference, for example CUS-123456, and the last four digits only.

## Who handles what
The employee who receives the complaint records it and tries to resolve simple matters immediately. If the matter needs investigation, the employee passes it to the branch supervisor, who assigns an owner. The branch manager is responsible for the overall quality of complaint handling in the branch and receives a weekly summary of open complaints.

Complaints about a branch manager, or complaints alleging fraud or misconduct, must be passed directly to the central complaints unit without discussion in the branch.

## Recording a complaint
Every complaint must be recorded on the day it is received with a reference number given to the customer. Complaints are acknowledged within 2 working days and resolved within 15 working days. The 15 working days are counted from the day the complaint was recorded, not from the day it was assigned to an owner.

Recording on the day of receipt applies even if the complaint arrives late in the afternoon. If the complaint is received after the branch closes, it is recorded at the start of the next working day and the date of receipt is noted in the record.

## Information to capture
The complaint record must contain:
- The customer reference and the customer's preferred contact method.
- The date and time the complaint was received and the channel used.
- A clear description in the customer's own words, followed by a short summary by the employee.
- The product or service involved, using only the last four digits of any card or account.
- What the customer is asking for, such as a refund, a correction or an apology.
- The name of the employee who recorded it.

## Giving the reference number
1. Create the record in the complaints log before the customer leaves, or before ending the call.
2. Read or hand over the reference number and ask the customer to keep it.
3. Explain the next steps and the time limits in plain language.
4. For written complaints, send the reference number to the customer on the day of recording.

## Acknowledgement
The acknowledgement confirms that the Bank has received the complaint. It must include the reference number, the name of the contact person in the branch, and the date by which the Bank expects to resolve the matter. A verbal acknowledgement given at the counter counts, provided it is noted in the record. All other complaints must be acknowledged in writing or by message within 2 working days.

If the customer gives a phone number, the employee may confirm receipt by text message, but the message must not contain any account details.

## Investigating a complaint
The case owner investigates the complaint objectively. The owner reviews the transaction history, the service request history and any notes made at the time of the event. The owner may speak to the employees involved but must not ask them to change records.

Investigation steps:
1. Read the complaint record and confirm the customer's request.
2. Collect the facts from systems and from staff involved.
3. Decide whether the complaint is justified, partly justified or not justified.
4. Decide on the remedy, such as a correction, a refund of a wrongly charged fee, or an apology.
5. Obtain approval for the remedy from the supervisor where it involves money or an account change.

## Remedies and approvals
Corrections that change an account or a card, such as reversing a fee, are carried out through a service request, which must be approved under the Service Request Approval Policy before execution. The case owner must not carry out a correction outside this process even when the customer is waiting.

Compensation of any kind is decided only by the branch manager. Employees must not offer compensation, gifts or fee waivers on their own authority.

## Keeping the customer informed
If the investigation will take longer than expected, the case owner must contact the customer before the planned resolution date, explain the delay, and give a new date. The new date must still fall within 15 working days unless the matter has been escalated. Each contact with the customer is added to the complaint record with the date and the name of the employee.

## Common difficult situations
- An angry customer: stay calm, use a lower voice, and move to a quiet area if possible.
- A customer who refuses to give details: record what is known and explain that resolution may be limited.
- A complaint made by a relative: share nothing about the account unless the account holder has given written permission.
- A complaint that includes threats: inform the branch manager immediately.

## Escalation
Complaints not resolved within 15 working days are escalated to the branch manager and the central complaints unit. The case owner must prepare the escalation on the 15th working day, at the latest, and must not wait for the customer to ask.

The escalation note must include the reference number, the original complaint, every action taken, the reasons for the delay, and the remedy the owner recommends. The branch manager reviews the note and confirms the escalation to the central complaints unit on the same working day.

## Early escalation
Some complaints do not wait for the 15 working day limit and are escalated at once to the branch manager and the central complaints unit. These include:
- Allegations of fraud, theft or staff misconduct.
- Complaints that mention legal action or a regulator.
- Complaints involving a vulnerable customer.
- Complaints about a risk of financial loss that is still continuing.

## After escalation
The central complaints unit takes over the investigation and informs the customer of the new contact person. The branch continues to support the unit by providing records and answering questions within 2 working days. The customer must be told that the complaint has been escalated, and what to expect next.

## Closing a complaint
1. Confirm that the agreed remedy has been completed.
2. Inform the customer of the outcome in writing or by the customer's preferred method.
3. Ask the customer whether the outcome is acceptable and record the answer.
4. Mark the complaint closed in the log with the closing date and a short summary of the outcome.

A complaint may not be closed only because the customer stopped responding. The case owner must make at least two attempts to contact the customer on different days before closing the record for lack of response.

## Records and monitoring
All complaint records are kept for at least five years. Records are confidential and may be viewed only by staff with a business need. Employees must not copy complaint details into personal notes or messaging applications.

Each month the branch manager reviews the complaints log and checks the following measures:
- The number of complaints recorded and closed.
- How many were acknowledged within 2 working days.
- How many were resolved within 15 working days.
- How many were escalated and why.
- The most frequent causes, by product and by channel.

## Learning from complaints
Recurring causes are reported to the Customer Experience Department each quarter. Branch managers must propose corrective actions for repeated causes, such as extra staff training or a change in a branch routine. Positive feedback received from customers is also recorded and shared with the staff concerned.

## Training and conduct
New employees must read this procedure and complete complaint handling training within their first month. Supervisors review a sample of complaint records every quarter to check that records are complete and the time limits were met.

Employees who ignore a complaint, delay recording it, or pressure a customer to withdraw it may face disciplinary action under the Bank's code of conduct.

## Summary of time limits
- Record the complaint: on the same day it is received, with a reference number for the customer.
- Acknowledge the complaint: within 2 working days.
- Resolve the complaint: within 15 working days.
- Escalate unresolved complaints: after 15 working days, to the branch manager and the central complaints unit.

## Quick checklist for the employee
1. Listen and thank the customer.
2. Record the complaint and give the reference number.
3. Explain the time limits.
4. Pass the case to the supervisor if it cannot be solved at once.
5. Follow up until the customer has an answer.

Questions about this procedure should be sent to the Customer Experience Department through the branch supervisor.
