# KYC Requirements for Account Opening
Synthetic training material. Not an official policy of any bank.
Document owner: Compliance and Retail Operations
Version: 1
Effective date: 2026-01-20

## Purpose and scope

KYC means Know Your Customer. This document sets out the identification and documentation requirements that branch employees of the Bank must apply when opening an account for an individual customer. It applies to current accounts, savings accounts and foreign currency accounts opened at a branch.

The purpose of KYC is to confirm who the customer is, where the customer lives, and how the customer earns a living. These checks protect the customer and the Bank against fraud, identity misuse and financial crime. They are mandatory. Employees may not skip or relax them to save time or to help a regular customer.

## Structure of this document

- Page 2 lists the mandatory documents for individuals.
- Page 3 explains how each document is checked.
- Page 4 explains what to do when documents are missing.
- Page 5 covers minors and their guardians.
- Page 6 covers completing the opening and keeping records.

## Roles

The employee who meets the customer collects and checks the documents, and prepares any service request. A supervisor approves requests before they are executed. The compliance team may review files at any time, so records must be complete and accurate.

## General rules

- Always see the original documents. Photocopies and phone photos are not accepted as originals.
- Identity must be verified before any account information is discussed.
- Write only the last four digits of any card or account number in notes. Never record full numbers.
- Customer references use the format CUS-123456, for example CUS-551907.
- Do not accept documents that are expired, damaged, altered or unreadable.
- If something looks suspicious, do not accuse the customer. Politely pause the process and inform your supervisor.

## Before the customer arrives

If the customer books an appointment, tell them in advance which documents to bring. The list is on the next page. This avoids a wasted visit and reduces the number of additional documentation requests.

## Mandatory documents for individuals

To open an individual account the customer must provide a valid national ID or passport, proof of address issued within the last three months, and proof of income or occupation. Minors require a guardian present with a birth certificate.

The three mandatory documents are explained below, with examples of what is normally accepted.

## Identity document

A valid national ID or passport. The document must be original, unexpired and in good condition. The photo must clearly match the customer. Foreign nationals use a passport together with a valid residence permit where applicable.

## Proof of address

The proof of address must be issued within the last three months. Typical examples:

- A utility bill for electricity, water, gas or landline.
- A bank statement from another institution showing the address.
- A rental contract registered with the relevant authority, if issued within the period.
- A letter from the employer or a university that states the address.

A document issued earlier than three months before the visit is not accepted, even if the customer says the address has not changed. The name on the document must match the name on the identity document, or the customer must explain the difference and the employee must record it.

## Proof of income or occupation

The customer must show how he or she earns a living. Typical examples:

- A recent salary statement or a payslip.
- An employment letter on company letterhead.
- A commercial register extract or tax card for self-employed customers.
- A student card or enrolment letter for students.
- A pension certificate for retired customers.

## Quick reference

- Identity: national ID or passport, valid.
- Address: issued within the last three months.
- Income or occupation: any accepted proof.
- Minors: guardian present with a birth certificate.

All three documents are needed. A customer who has two out of three cannot open the account on the same day.

## How documents are verified

Collecting documents is not enough. Each document must be checked, and the check must be recorded. This page describes the checks in the order in which they should be done.

## Step by step

1. Ask the customer to hand over the original identity document.
2. Check the expiry date. Reject the document if it is expired.
3. Compare the photo with the customer and the signature with the signature card being created.
4. Check the personal data against the Bank records if the customer already has a relationship with the Bank.
5. Check the issue date of the proof of address. It must fall within the last three months from the visit date.
6. Check that the name on the proof of address matches the identity document.
7. Review the proof of income or occupation and note its type in the file.
8. Scan or copy the documents as the system requires and return the originals to the customer.

## Red flags

Pause the process and inform your supervisor if you notice any of the following:

- Signs of alteration, such as different fonts, misaligned text or removed laminate.
- A photo that does not clearly match the customer.
- Documents that appear to have been issued on the same day with identical formatting for different customers.
- A customer who is unable to answer simple questions about the occupation described in the documents.

## What not to do

- Do not accept a document only because the customer is known to you.
- Do not accept a verbal confirmation of address or income.
- Do not keep the original documents after copying.

## Recording the check

In the file, note the type of each document, the issue date of the proof of address, the date of the check and the name of the employee who did it. This record will be used by the compliance team in later reviews.

## Incomplete documentation

If any mandatory document is missing, the account must not be opened. The employee records an additional documentation request listing the missing documents and a due date no later than 14 days from the visit.

This rule has no exceptions at branch level. A manager may not authorise the opening of an account with missing documents.

## How to record the request

1. Identify exactly which documents are missing or not acceptable, for example a proof of address older than three months.
2. Explain to the customer in plain words what is missing and why the account cannot be opened yet.
3. Create an additional documentation request in the request system.
4. Enter the customer reference, for example CUS-551907.
5. List every missing document by name. At least one document must be listed.
6. Set the due date. It must not be in the past, and it must be no later than 14 days from the visit.
7. Submit the request for supervisor approval.

## Choosing the due date

The due date is the latest day by which the customer should return with the documents. A shorter period may be agreed if the customer prefers. A period longer than 14 days is not allowed, and the system shows a warning if the date is beyond that limit.

Example: for a visit on 10 June, the due date can be 24 June at the latest.

## After the request is approved

Once the supervisor approves the request, it is executed once and a documentation reminder is scheduled. Tell the customer the due date and the list of missing documents. Give the customer a written note if the branch has the form.

## If the customer does not return

If the due date passes without the documents, the request stays open in the history and the customer must start the opening process again. Do not reuse old copies of documents for a new visit if they are no longer within the required period.

## Minors and guardians

Minors require a guardian present with a birth certificate. A minor is any customer below the age of legal majority under the law. This page explains the extra requirements for minor accounts.

## Who may act as guardian

The guardian is normally a parent. Another person may act as guardian only if there is a valid court decision or a legal document that names the person as guardian. The guardian must be present at the branch in person.

## Documents required for a minor account

- The original birth certificate of the minor.
- A valid national ID or passport of the guardian.
- The guardian's proof of address issued within the last three months.
- The guardian's proof of income or occupation.
- A national ID or passport of the minor, if the minor has one.
- A court decision or legal document, if the guardian is not a parent.

## Rules for minor accounts

1. The guardian must be present and sign on behalf of the minor.
2. The identity checks on page 3 apply to the guardian in full.
3. The birth certificate must be an original and must name the guardian as a parent, unless a court decision applies.
4. If the guardian is missing any mandatory document, the account must not be opened and an additional documentation request is recorded in the same way as for adults. The due date is no later than 14 days from the visit.
5. The minor account is linked to the guardian customer reference.

## Restrictions

Minor accounts have limited services. Debit cards, cheque books and credit products are not issued to minors under this document. Withdrawals above the limits agreed at opening may require the guardian to be present again.

## Reaching majority

When the minor reaches the age of majority, the account must be reviewed. The customer must visit the branch with a valid national ID, provide the three mandatory documents, and sign new account documents before the guardian link is removed.

## Completing the account opening

When all mandatory documents have been checked and are acceptable, the employee completes the opening. This page covers the final steps and the records that must be kept.

## Final steps

1. Confirm that the identity document, proof of address and proof of income or occupation are all present and valid.
2. Confirm that the supervisor has approved any related service request.
3. Complete the customer profile with the correct name, address, occupation and contact details.
4. Capture the customer's signature on the signature card.
5. Explain the account terms and the fees in the Retail Fee Schedule 2026, including monthly maintenance where it applies.
6. Hand the original documents back to the customer.
7. Give the customer the account reference, using only the last four digits when speaking about the account number.

## Records to keep

- Scanned copies of the identity document, proof of address and proof of income or occupation.
- The date and the name of the employee who checked the documents.
- Any additional documentation request and its due date.
- The supervisor approval, where one was required.

## Privacy

Customer documents are confidential. Do not send them through personal messaging applications and do not store them on personal devices. Do not write full card or account numbers in free-text notes. Only the last four digits may be recorded.

## Periodic review

Customer files are reviewed periodically by the compliance team. If a document on file has expired, the customer is contacted to provide a new one. Employees should explain this to customers who ask why the Bank requests updated documents.

## Summary

- Three mandatory documents for individuals.
- Proof of address within the last three months.
- No account opening with missing documents.
- Additional documentation request with a due date no later than 14 days from the visit.
- Guardian and birth certificate for minors.
