## Setting up a standing order

A standing order requires the beneficiary account number, amount, frequency, and start date. Standing orders to accounts at other banks are processed through ACH and may take one working day.

A standing order is a repeating instruction from the customer to move a fixed amount in EGP from the customer's account to a beneficiary. Typical uses are rent, school fees, loan repayments to another bank, or a monthly saving transfer.

Steps for the employee:

1. Verify the identity of the customer with an original national ID or passport.
2. Confirm that the paying account is active and has enough funds for the first payment. A dormant account cannot be debited until it is reactivated.
3. Collect the four required details: the beneficiary account number, the amount in EGP, the frequency (for example monthly or quarterly), and the start date.
4. Read back the beneficiary name shown by the system and ask the customer to confirm it. This reduces the risk of payments to the wrong person.
5. For a beneficiary at another bank, tell the customer that the transfer goes through ACH and may take one working day to reach the beneficiary. Transfers inside the Bank are normally credited on the same day.
6. Create the service request under the customer reference, for example CUS-277384, and submit it for supervisor approval. Supervisor approval is required for all service requests before execution.

The beneficiary account number is needed to set up the order, but it must not be copied into free-text notes in full. Use only the last 4 digits when referring to it, for example the beneficiary account ending 6052.

Fees for standing orders are listed in the retail fee schedule. Check them before the visit ends and tell the customer.

If the start date falls on a weekend or public holiday, the first payment is made on the next working day.

## Amending and cancelling standing orders

Customers may change the amount or frequency, or cancel a standing order. Each change is a service request that needs supervisor approval before execution.

Use these steps:

1. Verify the customer identity as for a new order.
2. Locate the standing order on the account screen and read out the current details: amount, frequency, next payment date and the last 4 digits of the beneficiary account.
3. Ask what should change. A change of beneficiary is not an amendment. It must be treated as a cancellation of the existing order and a new order for the new beneficiary.
4. Create the service request, state clearly in the description whether it is an amendment or a cancellation, and submit it for approval.
5. Tell the customer the date from which the change takes effect.

Timing is important. A change that is approved after the payment run for the day does not stop that day's payment. If the next payment is due tomorrow, tell the customer that the change might apply only from the following payment, and let the supervisor confirm.

For orders to other banks processed through ACH, a payment already sent cannot be recalled by the branch employee. The customer must contact the beneficiary, or a dispute must be raised through the supervisor.

Example: customer CUS-143908 wants to stop a monthly rent payment of EGP 4,000 starting next month. The employee checks the next run date, raises a cancellation request, and records the service reference for the customer.

Never promise a cancellation before the supervisor has approved it.

## Failed payments and escalation

A standing order payment may fail for several reasons:

- Insufficient funds in the paying account on the payment date.
- The paying account is dormant, blocked or restricted.
- The beneficiary account at another bank is closed or the details are wrong, so ACH returns the payment.
- The amount exceeds a limit applicable to the account.

When a customer reports a failed payment:

1. Check the standing order history on the account screen and identify the reason shown.
2. If funds were insufficient, advise the customer to fund the account. Do not run the payment again by hand. The next scheduled run will follow the rules of the order, and any retry needs a new service request approved by a supervisor.
3. If ACH returned the payment, confirm the beneficiary details with the customer and, if needed, create a new order or amendment.
4. If the customer says a payment was made that they did not authorise, treat it as a dispute, tell the supervisor, and follow the complaints handling procedure by recording the complaint on the same day.

Remind customers that transfers to other banks may take one working day, so a payment sent on Thursday may reach the beneficiary only on Sunday, depending on the weekend schedule. Do not assume that a payment is lost before that time has passed.

Escalate to the supervisor when three consecutive payments fail, when the beneficiary is a person the customer does not recognise, or when the amounts or frequency look unusual for the customer's profile. In all notes, use the customer reference and the last 4 digits only.
