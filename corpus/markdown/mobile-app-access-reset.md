## Password reset

Customers who forget their mobile app password can reset it from the app using their card details and a one-time password. If the customer is locked out after five failed attempts, a branch employee must raise a digital banking request to unlock the profile.

Whenever possible, encourage the customer to use the self-service reset so that the branch workload stays low and the customer learns the process. Guide the customer through these steps without touching the phone or asking for the password:

1. In the app, select Forgot password on the login screen.
2. Enter the debit card details requested by the app. The employee must not write down or repeat the card number; only the last 4 digits may appear in any note.
3. Enter the one-time password sent by SMS to the registered mobile number.
4. Choose a new password that is different from the previous one.

Staff must never ask a customer for the app password, the one-time password or the PIN, even when trying to help. If the one-time password does not arrive, check that the registered mobile number on the record is correct and that the customer has signal. If the number on the record is wrong, the customer must update it at the branch with a national ID.

Example: a customer with reference CUS-402917 reports that the reset SMS never arrives. The employee confirms the registered number ends with the digits the customer expects, finds that it is outdated, and starts a registered mobile update request for supervisor approval.

If the self-service reset fails repeatedly, move to the locked profile process in the final section of this guide.

## Device change

When a customer changes their phone, the mobile app must be re-registered on the new device. The registered mobile number must remain the same; changing the registered mobile number requires a branch visit with a national ID.

Device change applies to the mobile app channel only and cannot be selected for internet banking requests. Follow these steps:

1. Ask the customer whether the old phone is lost or stolen. If so, treat it as a security concern and note it in the case.
2. Confirm the SIM with the registered number is in the new phone.
3. Ask the customer to install the official app from the store and begin registration.
4. If the customer cannot complete registration alone, create a digital banking request of type device change under the customer reference, for example CUS-118820.
5. Submit the request for supervisor approval. Supervisor approval is required for all service requests before execution.

If the customer has a new SIM with a new number, the case is a registered mobile number change, not a simple device change. Verify the original national ID, update the number, and only then re-register the device. Remind the customer that the app on the old phone will stop working once the new device is registered.

If the old phone was lost, advise the customer to request removal of the old device registration and to change the password after the new device is active.

## Locked profile and unlock requests

The mobile app locks after five failed login attempts. The customer cannot unlock the profile alone, and the employee must raise a digital banking request.

Use these steps:

1. Verify the identity of the customer with an original national ID or passport.
2. Check the registered mobile number on the record.
3. Create a digital banking request for the mobile app channel with the issue type for a locked profile, under the customer reference, for example CUS-560231.
4. Enter the registered mobile number in the 11-digit format. The validation will reject any other format.
5. Submit the request. A supervisor must approve it before the unlock is executed; no unlock may be done earlier, even if the customer is waiting.
6. After execution, note the ticket reference and tell the customer that a new one-time password will be sent by SMS.

Advise the customer to avoid guessing passwords, because each wrong attempt counts toward the lock. Repeated locks within a short period may signal that someone else is trying to enter the profile. In that case, tell the supervisor, and recommend that the customer change the password straight after the unlock.

A request must be executed only once. If the customer returns saying the unlock did not work, check the original ticket first and escalate to the digital banking team rather than raising a duplicate request.
