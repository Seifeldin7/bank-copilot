# Dormant Account Reactivation

Synthetic training material. Not an official policy of any bank.

Document owner: Retail Accounts Department
Version: 1
Effective date: 2026-03-01

## Definition of dormancy

An account becomes dormant after 12 consecutive months without customer-initiated transactions. Dormant accounts cannot be debited until they are reactivated.

A customer-initiated transaction is any action started by the customer, such as a cash deposit or withdrawal at the branch, an ATM withdrawal, a card purchase, a transfer sent from internet banking or the mobile app, or a cheque issued by the customer. Actions started by the Bank do not count. These include interest credits, fee debits, standing order failures, and statement or maintenance charges. An account that only receives interest and pays fees for 12 months is still dormant.

What the employee will see:

- A dormant flag on the account screen, shown next to the account status.
- Declined debits, card purchases and transfers out of the account.
- Incoming credits may still be accepted, but they do not make the account active again.

What the employee must tell the customer:

1. The funds are safe and still belong to the customer.
2. Money can be received, but cannot be withdrawn or sent until the account is reactivated.
3. Reactivation is free of charge in this procedure unless the retail fee schedule says otherwise.

Example: customer CUS-845102 asks why the card ending 2290 was declined at a shop. The employee checks the linked account ending 7731, sees that the last customer-initiated transaction was 14 months ago and that the account is dormant. The employee explains the rule and starts the reactivation process in the next section.

Do not tell the customer that the account is closed. A dormant account is different from a closed account.

## Reactivation requirements

To reactivate a dormant account the customer must visit the branch with a valid national ID and update their contact details. Reactivation is an account service request and requires supervisor approval.

Reactivation cannot be done by phone, chat or through a third party without a formal authority process. If someone else comes to the branch on behalf of the customer, refer the case to the supervisor and do not start the request.

Required items:

1. A valid original national ID or passport. Expired documents are not accepted.
2. Updated contact details: mobile number, address and email where available.
3. A signature compared against the signature on the Bank records.

Check the contact details carefully. A wrong mobile number will block internet banking enrollment and the delivery of one-time passwords later. If the customer wants to change the registered mobile number, verify the national ID and record the change as part of the same visit.

Prepare the account service request with the customer reference, for example CUS-845102, the account type, and the last 4 digits of the account only. Add a short description that the account is dormant and that the customer attended in person. Submit the request. The supervisor can approve, correct or reject it, and a rejection must have a reason.

The employee must not make any debit or transfer on the account before the approval and execution. If the customer is waiting to withdraw cash, explain the timeline and do not promise the exact time of execution.

## Reactivation steps and follow-up

Follow these steps in order:

1. Verify the customer identity under the Customer Identification and Verification Standard.
2. Confirm the account is dormant and not frozen, blocked or under legal restriction. Frozen or restricted accounts cannot be handled with this process, and the supervisor must be told.
3. Update the contact details on the customer record.
4. Create the account service request for reactivation and submit it for supervisor approval. Supervisor approval is required for all service requests before execution.
5. After the request is approved and executed, check that the dormant flag is removed from the account screen.
6. Tell the customer that the account can now be debited and give the service reference number.

Executed requests cannot be repeated. If the flag is still shown after execution, do not create a second request; contact the supervisor, who will check the original execution record.

Follow-up advice for the customer:

- Make at least one customer-initiated transaction soon after reactivation, such as a small deposit or ATM withdrawal, to restart the 12 month period clearly.
- Cards and digital banking profiles linked to the account may need separate attention. A card that expired during the dormant period needs a replacement request. An internet banking profile that was inactive may need a new activation code, which expires after 24 hours.

Escalate to the supervisor if the account has been dormant for several years, if the balance is unusually large for the customer profile, or if the customer cannot explain the inactivity and the identity documents do not match the records.
