# Lost and Stolen Card Handling

Synthetic training material. Not an official policy of any bank.

Document owner: Cards and Payments Department
Version: 1
Effective date: 2026-01-15

## Immediate blocking

When a customer reports a lost or stolen card, the employee must block the card immediately through the card management screen and give the customer the blocking reference number. Blocking is permanent and the card cannot be reactivated.

Blocking is the first action in every lost or stolen card case. Do not delay it to complete other paperwork, and do not wait for supervisor approval to block, because blocking protects the customer from further loss. Follow these steps:

1. Verify the identity of the customer under the Customer Identification and Verification Standard.
2. Open the card management screen using the customer reference, for example CUS-204518, and select the correct card by its last 4 digits only.
3. Choose the reason: lost or stolen.
4. Confirm the block and read the blocking reference number to the customer. Ask the customer to write it down.
5. Record the time of the block in the case notes. This time decides whether later transactions are treated as disputed.

Tell the customer clearly that the block cannot be undone. If the customer later finds the card, it must be destroyed and a replacement must be requested. Never promise that a blocked card can be switched back on.

If the card management screen is unavailable, ask the customer to call the card hotline immediately and note the call time. Then block the card in the system as soon as it is available and record the reason for the delay.

Never ask the customer to say the full card number aloud in the branch. The last 4 digits are enough to identify the card.

## Stolen cards and disputed transactions

For stolen cards, the customer should file a police report within 48 hours if disputed transactions occurred. Disputed transactions after the blocking time are refunded after investigation, which takes up to 30 days.

Explain these points to the customer in plain words:

- The 48 hour period applies to stolen cards where the customer claims transactions they did not make. A lost card with no disputed transactions does not need a police report.
- Only transactions made after the blocking time are eligible for refund. Transactions made before the block are examined during the investigation but are not refunded automatically.
- The investigation takes up to 30 days. The customer should not expect an instant credit, and employees must not promise the outcome.

Example: customer CUS-731206 reports that a card ending 4417 was stolen on a bus and that three withdrawals appear on the statement. The employee blocks the card, notes the block time, lists the three withdrawals, and reminds the customer to bring the police report reference within 48 hours. The employee records the police report reference in the notes of the card service request. If the reference is not yet available, the request may be prepared but the employee must note that the report is pending.

Direct the customer to the complaints procedure if they are unhappy with the investigation result. Never record the full card number in the dispute notes.

## Replacement after blocking

A customer whose card was blocked as lost or stolen may ask for a replacement. Replacement is a card service request and needs supervisor approval before it is executed, as for all service requests.

Prepare the request as follows:

1. Confirm that the old card is blocked and keep the blocking reference number.
2. Record the last 4 digits of the old card and the customer reference, for example CUS-204518.
3. Select the issue type lost or stolen and enter the delivery branch code, for example CAI-012.
4. Add a note stating whether a police report is expected. For stolen cards with disputes, include the police report reference when available.
5. Tell the customer that the card replacement fee of EGP 50 applies as listed in the retail fee schedule, and that it is debited from the linked account after the request is approved.
6. Submit the request for supervisor approval.

The new card is ready for collection within 7 working days at the chosen delivery branch. The customer must collect it in person with an original national ID or passport. The PIN is set separately by the customer and must never be written by staff.

Warn the customer that the old card details no longer work on subscriptions and online services, and that recurring payments linked to the old card must be updated with each merchant.

## Escalation and records

Escalate to the supervisor immediately when any of the following happens:

- The customer reports transactions made after the block time. This may mean the block failed.
- The customer says a staff member or relative knew the PIN or took the card.
- More than one card is reported lost or stolen in the same visit.
- The customer cannot be identified with an original document. In this case do not block by phone, and refer to the Customer Identification and Verification Standard.
- The customer refuses to file a police report for a stolen card with disputed transactions. Record the refusal and inform the supervisor.

Records to keep for every case:

1. The customer reference, such as CUS-204518.
2. The last 4 digits of the card only.
3. The blocking reference number and the block time.
4. The service request number for any replacement.
5. The police report reference, if one applies.

Do not write full card numbers, PINs, or card security codes in the notes, in emails, or in chat messages. If the customer shows a photo of the card on their phone, ask them to hide the number before you look at it.

At the end of the visit, give the customer the blocking reference number, the expected time for the replacement, and the dispute timeline. Repeat that refund decisions can take up to 30 days. If the customer calls back, check the case history before giving an answer and avoid giving different information from the first visit.
