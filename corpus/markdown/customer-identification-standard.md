# Customer Identification and Verification Standard

Synthetic training material. Not an official policy of any bank.

Document owner: Compliance Department
Version: 1
Effective date: 2026-05-04

## Verification before service

Before providing any account information, the employee must verify the customer identity using an original national ID or passport and compare the photo and signature with the bank records.

This rule applies to every visit, including for long-standing customers whom the employee knows personally. It applies before the employee answers questions about balances, transactions, limits or card status, and before any service request is prepared.

Steps:

1. Ask the customer for an original document. Photocopies, photos on a phone and scanned images are not accepted.
2. Check that the document is valid and not expired, and that it is not visibly damaged or altered.
3. Compare the face of the customer with the photo on the document.
4. Compare the name and the national ID number with the customer record, which is found using the customer reference, for example CUS-481026.
5. Ask the customer to sign on a blank slip and compare the signature with the signature on the Bank records.
6. Record in the case notes that the check was done and which document type was used. Do not copy the full ID number into free-text notes.

Only after these checks may the employee tell the customer anything about the account. If one check fails, stop and follow the section on mismatches and suspicious cases.

Verification is done for each service request. A supervisor reviewing a service request expects to see that identity was verified, and may reject the request if the record does not show it.

Do not verify a customer by asking questions such as date of birth only. Such details can be known by other people and are not a replacement for the original document.

## Acceptable documents

The following documents are accepted as proof of identity for individuals:

- A valid Egyptian national ID card.
- A valid passport. For foreign nationals, a passport is the primary document, and the supervisor should be told if a residence permit is also needed for the service.

Not accepted as proof of identity: driving licences, work cards, club cards, student cards, and bank cards. They may be kept as supporting items but do not replace the national ID or passport.

For account opening, further documents are needed, such as proof of address issued within the last three months and proof of income or occupation. Minors need a guardian present with a birth certificate. If a mandatory document is missing, the account must not be opened. The employee records an additional documentation request listing the missing documents and a due date no later than 14 days from the visit.

Example: customer CUS-559310 arrives to request a statement and offers only a photo of the ID on a phone. The employee explains that an original document is required, politely declines to proceed and invites the customer to return with it.

Expired documents: if the national ID expired, the customer cannot receive service that needs verification. The employee should explain how to renew it and note the visit. Ask the supervisor whether any urgent service is allowed, such as blocking a lost card, which should never be delayed.

## Mismatches and suspicious cases

Treat the following as warning signs:

- The photo does not look like the person present.
- The signature differs clearly from the Bank record.
- The document looks altered, or the details differ from the customer record.
- The person is nervous, rushed or reads details from a phone message while trying to obtain a service.
- A third party is directing the customer or answering for them.
- The customer asks for information about another person's account.

What to do:

1. Do not accuse the customer. Say that the check cannot be completed and that a supervisor will help.
2. Do not carry out the service or share any account information, even a little.
3. Call the supervisor and let them examine the document.
4. Record the facts in neutral words: what did not match, the time, and the customer reference.
5. If the supervisor suspects fraud, follow the Bank escalation steps, and do not discuss the suspicion with the customer.

Example: a person with reference CUS-702115 asks to reactivate a dormant account but the signature on the slip differs from the Bank record. The employee stops, calls the supervisor, and does not start the request.

Never override a failed check because the customer is in a hurry or because of the amount involved. Pressure to skip verification is itself a warning sign.

Exceptions for emergencies, such as immediate card blocking, must be approved by a supervisor, and identity must still be verified as soon as possible afterwards.

## Record keeping and repeat checks

Keep a short and accurate record of each verification, without storing more personal data than needed.

What to record:

1. The customer reference, for example CUS-481026.
2. The document type checked: national ID or passport.
3. The date, time and employee identifier.
4. The outcome: passed, failed, or referred to the supervisor.

What not to record: the full ID number, full card or account numbers, or images of documents on personal devices. Use the last 4 digits when a number must be referred to. Copies of documents may be taken only where a procedure requires it, and they go into the approved customer file, not into e-mail, chat or personal devices.

When to repeat verification:

- At each visit, even if the customer was verified last week.
- When the customer returns after leaving the counter during a service, if the employee cannot be sure the same person returned.
- When a different person collects a card, cheque book or certificate. The collecting person must be verified, and must be authorised.
- When the customer asks to change contact details, because these are used for one-time passwords and activation codes.

Service requests that depend on the verification, such as card replacement, dormant account reactivation or digital banking unlocks, need supervisor approval before execution. The supervisor relies on the notes you made, so they must be clear and complete.

Records are reviewed during branch audits. Missing verification notes are treated as a control failure, so complete them on the same day. If you find a record from an earlier visit without a verification note, tell the supervisor instead of adding one afterwards.
